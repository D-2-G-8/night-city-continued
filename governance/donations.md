# Donations

> **Status: stage A is not open yet.** This file records the policy that has already been
> decided, so that code and reviews have something to point at. No money is being collected
> at the time of writing. Stage A starts at step 4 of the launch plan; stage B is gated on a
> written answer from CD Projekt Red, and that letter has not been sent.

## The rule everything else follows from

**Nothing in this project is ever behind money.**

Not a feature, not a build, not a channel, not support, not a place on the roadmap. This is
not a fundraising tactic — it is what keeps the project inside CD Projekt Red's position on
fan work and inside Nexus Mods' rules, and it is what makes it safe for an author to bring
their mod here.

## What money may not buy

1. **No privileges.** No early access, no donor-only builds or features, no exclusive
   content, no weight in polls or on the roadmap, no priority support, no faster review.
   Every channel — nightly, beta, stable — opens to everyone at the same moment.
2. **No strings attached to content.** Money offered on condition that something appears in
   the game is refused, whatever the amount and whatever it is called. "Add brand X and we
   will donate" is a refusal, every time.
3. **No advertising and no paid product placement**, in the game or in the launcher.
4. **No payment for a future feature.** Paying to have something built is how stretch goals
   work, and stretch goals for mod development are forbidden by Nexus. Grants recognise work
   already done (see below).
5. **No mod, update, file or support in exchange for a donation.** Also a Nexus rule.

The only thing a donor may receive is a mention in the credits, if they want one.

## Where the money goes

**Money never passes through the founder's personal account.** It goes through a fiscal host
with a public ledger (Open Collective is the candidate) [verify host availability and payouts
for contributors' countries].

### Stage A — infrastructure only

Opens at step 4 of the launch plan. Funds:

- lab hardware,
- hosting and CI,
- a code-signing certificate for the launcher.

Alongside it, a **contributors page** links to each author's own donation channels. Those
donations go directly to the author and never touch project funds.

A public report goes out monthly: income, spending, and what it bought.

### Stage B — the shared fund

**Does not open** until CD Projekt Red has confirmed in writing that a shared fund
distributed among contributors is acceptable. That letter is deferred by decision of the
project owner, so stage B is closed indefinitely and not a near-term plan.

It also does not open while there is no maintainer council, since the council — not the
founder — decides grants.

Opening allocation, if and when it opens:

| Line | Share |
|---|---|
| Infrastructure | 40% |
| Reserve | 10% |
| Quarterly contributor grants | 50% |

Until then, 100% of income goes to infrastructure and reserve.

## Grants (stage B)

- Decided by the **maintainer council** (3–5 people), never by the founder alone.
- A grant **recognises work already done** in the quarter. It is not payment for a future
  feature, and it cannot be promised in advance.
- **Public criteria:** maintaining modules and recipes after game patches, roadmap work,
  tooling and the lab, documentation, helping others port their mods, community work.
  **Art, writing, voice acting, translation and QA count equally with code.**
- Amounts and recipients are published with a short justification.
- **Founder's conflict of interest:** any payment to the founder is a separate line in the
  report and is approved by the council **without her vote**.
- **Taxes:** each recipient declares income in their own country; the fiscal host provides
  payout documentation.
- **Leaving the project:** paid grants are not returned. Rights to code are set by its
  license, not by fund policy.

## Authors keep what is theirs

Bringing a mod here never requires leaving Nexus, changing a license, or giving up donations.
Downloads of `linked` mods go through the author's own pages and count for them. An author
may drop their mod to a lower tier or remove the recipe at any time.
